Show how the effects of polluted air extend beyond the person who breathes it.

Smoke and exhaust harm people, but their effects spread further. Pollutants injure flower and vegetable crops. Soot darkens buildings and polluted air can damage their surfaces. Acid rain affects lakes and removes nutrients from soil, weakening forests and the creatures that depend on them. Wind can take pollution beyond the country where it began. The passage thus connects local emissions with wider environmental harm. ଜୀବ ଓ ଜଡ଼ ପଦାର୍ଥ ଉଭୟର ଉଦାହରଣ ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ।

3 · Essay · ରଚନା · 10 ନମ୍ବର

Write an essay in about 200 words on “The Unseen Cost of Smoke”.

The Unseen Cost of Smoke A chimney may look different a few minutes after fuel begins to burn. The cloud of smoke may seem to disappear, and a passer-by may suppose that the danger has passed too. The passage on air pollution asks the reader to look beyond what can be seen at one moment. Particles and gases can mix with the surrounding air and be carried elsewhere. People may breathe them in without noticing each entry into the body. Buildings can become dark with soot, and the surface of a monument can suffer damage. In a lake affected by acid rain the cost is felt by living things. On land, the loss of nutrients from the soil can weaken a forest and affect the creatures that live there. It is therefore important to ask where the pollution begins as well as where its effects appear. Cleaner fuels can prevent part of the damage. Equipment can catch pollutants before they leave a factory. Standards and research can help these measures improve. Because wind can cross borders, one community’s air is linked with another’s. Taking smoke seriously means considering the human and environmental costs that remain long after a cloud has vanished from sight.

Write an essay in about 200 words on “Listening to Two Voices”.

Listening to Two Voices A conversation can show us two different ideas of happiness more clearly than a list of opinions. In the village song, a mother and daughter both speak with feeling. Each offers images that help the reader understand what she values. The mother thinks of the care she has already given her child and the family life that lies ahead. The bridegroom is coming. Clothes are being woven and food is being prepared. Her questions are affectionate as well as urgent. She wants her daughter to recognise the people who will be hurt if she leaves. The daughter answers in images of the forest. She sees flowers opening and shining lilies by the water. Most importantly, she hears sounds that seem to call especially to her. She can also hear sorrow beneath the music of marriage and family life. For her, worldly pleasure carries the possibility of loss. Listening to both voices helps us understand why the conversation is difficult. The mother’s preparations express love, and the daughter’s answer expresses a powerful attraction to another kind of life. The reader hears each side directly and can appreciate the feelings that a simple command to stay would have left unheard.

Write an essay in about 200 words on “An Opportunity to Begin Again”.

An Opportunity to Begin Again A person who has lost a place in society may need more than a warning to change. Lushkoff has lost his singing work because of drinking. He begs by telling different stories about himself. His meeting with Sergei begins badly because the advocate recognises a lie. Sergei offers him work instead of merely sending him away. At first the task appears unsuitable: the man is weak, the weather is cold and the wood must be chopped. Olga, the cook, sees how little he can do. Although her words sound severe, she is moved to tears by his suffering and performs the work herself. Lushkoff later remembers this private kindness more deeply than any lecture. A second opportunity suits him better. On discovering that he can write, Sergei sends him to a friend for copying work. When the two meet again, Lushkoff has become a notary and can look back on the change in his life. He acknowledges the advocate’s help but also explains what happened in the shed. The story suggests that an opportunity succeeds when it meets a real need. Practical work, recognition of an existing skill and another person’s compassion can act together to make a new beginning possible.

Write about 100 words to a friend explaining why the end of Lencho’s story surprised you.

Cuttack 20 November Dear Rabi, I expected Lencho to thank the workers when he received money after the storm. Instead he asked God to send the balance by a different route because he suspected those very workers. What surprised me was the difference between his knowledge and mine. I had read how the postmaster collected the money, but Lencho saw only the name on the envelope and the amount inside it. His complete trust in God led him to a mistaken judgement of his neighbours. I would like to hear whether you expected that ending. Yours affectionately, Anita
