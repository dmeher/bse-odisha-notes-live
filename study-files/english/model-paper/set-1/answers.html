How do the three approaches to controlling air pollution differ? Illustrate each.

Prevention changes what goes into production: a cleaner fuel can reduce pollution at its source. Dispersal spreads an emission, for instance through a higher chimney. Collection uses equipment to catch a pollutant before it reaches the open air. Each acts at a different stage. Since wind can carry emissions across borders, these controls also need cooperation beyond a single factory or country. ତିନି ପଦ୍ଧତି କେଉଁ ସ୍ତରରେ କାମ କରେ ଦେଖାନ୍ତୁ।

Write an essay in about 200 words on “Learning Beyond the School Gate”.

Learning Beyond the School Gate Leaving school changes the place where we learn. It does not bring learning itself to an end. A pupil who begins a new occupation soon meets questions that a school lesson alone cannot answer. The habit of asking for advice and the willingness to work patiently become especially valuable then. The choice of work deserves care. A temporary job may be necessary, but a young person can continue to look for work that offers interest and a future. A trusted teacher can help the pupil weigh such a choice. Health also affects how far a person can carry out a decision. Regular habits, cleanliness, fresh air and suitable recreation give the body strength for work. Learning includes our use of time and our conduct towards people. A good book can occupy an idle hour. A considerate friend can influence our choices. Being fair to a rival or helping someone in distress gives practical meaning to the lessons of school. Success then includes more than obtaining employment. It includes character, courtesy and the ability to serve one’s family and country. The best farewell to school is to carry these habits into the wider world.

Write an essay in about 200 words on “Clean Air and a Shared Duty”.

Clean Air and a Shared Duty Air enters our bodies throughout the day. When smoke disappears from sight, it is tempting to think that it has disappeared altogether. Yet particles and harmful gases can remain mixed with the air around a home or a school. Industrial furnaces need fuel, and vehicles move people and goods from place to place. Both activities can release unwanted substances. Their effects extend beyond a blackened wall or a cough. Polluted air can damage plants, while acid rain can change water in lakes and remove nutrients from the soil. Birds and animals suffer when the forest on which they depend begins to die. There are several places at which action can be taken. A change of fuel can prevent some pollution. Equipment can trap a pollutant before it leaves a factory. Taller chimneys can disperse emissions, although dispersal does not remove their cause. Laws and standards help different users of fuel work towards cleaner air. Wind, however, does not stop at a national boundary. One area can receive the effects of another area’s choices. Protecting the air therefore calls for research, sustained effort and cooperation among people who share it.

What It Means to Help One kind of help is easy to count. A family whose crop has failed needs money to sow again and to buy food until the harvest.
