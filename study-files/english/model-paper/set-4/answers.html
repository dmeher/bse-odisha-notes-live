3 · Essay · ରଚନା · 10 ନମ୍ବର

Write an essay in about 200 words on “Leisure as a Part of Education”.

Leisure as a Part of Education The hours after lessons or work can appear unimportant because nobody has set a task for them. Yet the school farewell address treats leisure as one of the ways in which a person’s future is formed. A pupil who learns how to use free time has already gained a useful habit. A good book is one possibility. It allows the reader to keep learning when a teacher is no longer present. A hobby offers another kind of occupation and can provide the recreation needed for health. A companion can make such hours better or worse. A friend who encourages thoughtful choices has an influence that extends beyond a single afternoon. This does not mean that every leisure hour must feel like another class. The speaker values recreation because people need a sound body as well as a sound mind. The important question is what an activity does to our habits. Gambling encourages a person to seek gain at somebody else’s expense. Reading, a useful interest and good company can instead give lasting satisfaction. School will eventually end for every pupil. Free time will remain. Learning to use it well can therefore carry one part of education far beyond the school gate.

Write an essay in about 200 words on “An Ending That Changes a Story”.

An Ending That Changes a Story An ending can make a reader reconsider a scene that seemed clear a moment earlier. When Grandfather returns to the zoo, he approaches the cage where he left Timothy. His confidence is understandable because he discovered the tiger as a cub and watched it grow up in his home. The animal in the cage allows him to touch its head and even licks his hand. A leopard in the next cage frightens it. Grandfather’s attention goes immediately to the animal’s comfort, and he asks that it be moved. Anyone who has followed the history of the cub can easily accept this as a meeting between old companions. A keeper then explains that Timothy died of pneumonia two months earlier. The tiger in the cage was captured in the hills and is considered dangerous. Suddenly the animal’s behaviour takes on a different meaning. It had not been greeting a familiar friend. Grandfather has been closer to danger than he realised. The surprise works because the earlier details remain true. The cage was familiar, the animal did respond and Grandfather really did care. What changes is the meaning he and the reader gave those details. A single piece of information transforms the whole encounter.

Write an essay in about 200 words on “Learning from Experience”.

Learning from Experience A large grain of corn puzzles everyone who sees it. Its journey from children in a ravine to a king’s possession could have ended with its being kept as an interesting object. Instead, the king wants to know where and when grain of such a size grew. The wise men first try to solve the puzzle themselves. A hen helps reveal that the object is indeed a grain, but their books cannot tell them its history. They recognise the limits of their knowledge and suggest questioning the peasants. The first man remembers no grain like it. His father remembers larger grain than people see now but cannot identify the king’s specimen. The eldest man finally recognises it from the days when he grew and ate such corn. Each answer is valuable, even when it admits uncertainty. It leads the king closer to the person with direct experience. The oldest man also explains what labour, land and sharing meant in his youth. At that point the object becomes more than a curiosity. The story asks the reader to value knowledge gained through work and memory. Books begin the inquiry, but listening carefully to different generations gives the king the answer he came to seek.
