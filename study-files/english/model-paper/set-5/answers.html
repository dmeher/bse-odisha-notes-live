Write an essay in about 200 words on “The Dignity of Labour”.

The Dignity of Labour The eldest peasant in the story about a great grain of corn looks at work in a way that surprises the king. Asked where his land was, he recalls a time when land was freely cultivated and people claimed their labour as their own. The grain becomes a means of asking what one person may fairly expect from another. The grandfather remembers sowing, reaping and feeding others. Work connected people directly with what they ate. In his account they had enough and did not desire what a neighbour had produced. The comparison with the later generations brings out the importance he attaches to independence and restraint. His grandson depends on two crutches, while the grandfather himself walks easily. Within the story this visible contrast helps the king understand the old man’s moral argument. Labour is dignified here because it meets a real need without taking unfair advantage of someone else. It can also create enough for sharing. The idea is quite different from looking only at the price for which an unusual grain might be sold. By the end of the king’s inquiry, what matters most is no longer the size of the object. It is the kind of life that the oldest peasant remembers.

Choosing a Profession with Care The first job offered to a school-leaver may appear to answer every question about the future. In fact it answers only one question: what work is available now? The farewell message asks pupils to think also about what that work can become. An occupation worth choosing should offer some promise of interest and a satisfying future. A young person may not have enough experience to judge this alone. Asking a teacher for advice is therefore a sensible part of making an independent choice. Advice can draw attention to possibilities that excitement or anxiety has hidden. It does not remove the pupil’s responsibility to think carefully. Sometimes circumstances require a person to accept temporary work. The speaker recognises this difficulty. A temporary job can be taken while the person continues looking for a more suitable occupation. Once a better choice is found, learning must still continue. Good health supports steady work, while honesty and consideration guide the way that work is done. Write an essay in about 200 words on “Why Cooperation Matters for Clean Air”.

Why Cooperation Matters for Clean Air A factory stands in one place, and a city can count the vehicles using its roads. It may therefore seem natural to treat every problem of polluted air as a purely local matter. The account of acid rain shows why this view is incomplete. Once an emission enters the air, wind may carry it far beyond its source. The people who first notice damage to a lake or forest may live in another region or country. They can protect a local area in many ways, but they cannot solve the entire problem if the source remains unchanged elsewhere. The cost may also appear slowly as nutrients are washed from soil or stone surfaces deteriorate. Cooperation begins with understanding these connections. Factories can consider changes in fuel and equipment. Researchers from different fields can compare observations and seek better controls. Laws and standards can encourage action in places where emissions begin. A town that reduces its own pollution may be helping people it will never see. The need for cooperation does not remove local responsibility. It makes that responsibility clearer. When air is shared, the value of a practical improvement extends beyond the point at which the work is carried out.
